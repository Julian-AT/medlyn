\section{Exploratory quantum experiments}
\label{app:quantum}
We implement two synthetic-only experiments with IBM Qiskit, separate from the
clinical runtime and the main benchmark. Neither experiment establishes clinical
benefit or quantum advantage. The aggregate report is
\texttt{ml/quantum/results.json}; measurements remain automated and await human
review. We withhold performance values from this appendix until that review.

\paragraph{Claim classification.}
We sample 400 training and 200 test examples from the frozen verifier splits.
Patients and generation families remain disjoint. We balance supported claims
against the union of unsupported and contradicted claims. This binary task is not
comparable to the main three-class verifier evaluation. Pair features concatenate
cosine similarity, element-wise products and absolute differences of claim and
passage vectors. PCA and scaling fit only on training features. We encode eight
components in a fully connected ZZ feature map with two repetitions, then fit an
SVM to the fidelity kernel. RBF-SVM and logistic regression use identical features
and fixed settings. Patient bootstrap intervals are conditional on the sampled
families. These synthetic labels are code checked, not human gold.

\paragraph{Execution and feature provenance.}
The planned encoder is Gemini Embedding 2 with 768 dimensions. Its daily quota was
exhausted during this run. We therefore completed an explicitly labelled offline
control using character-hash vectors. The Gemini experiment remains pending.
The full simulator computes exact statevector overlaps; a small finite-shot block
cross-checks the same map with \texttt{FidelityQuantumKernel} and
\texttt{ComputeUncompute}. Exact overlaps avoid a quadratic number of separately
sampled circuits. This adjustment is recorded in the report. Same-subset LoRA and
GPT reference predictions are unavailable; we do not substitute earlier scores.

\paragraph{Evidence selection.}
For each of five synthetic patients we choose three of eight to ten candidates.
A QUBO maximises a lexical and embedding relevance surrogate minus pairwise
redundancy, with a cardinality penalty larger than the objective's absolute range.
We compare exhaustive search, greedy selection and QAOA at depths one, two and
three on Aer. The optimiser uses ideal statevector expectations and the final
circuit is sampled. Conditional approximation ratios, feasibility probability and
unconditional optimal-set probability are separate quantities. Postselection does
not establish reliable evidence selection. Exhaustive search is practical for
these instances, and the surrogate is not a clinical relevance standard.

\paragraph{Hardware and limitations.}
The lane did not submit a hardware job. A separate orchestrator runbook constructs
a 16 by 16 test-kernel block on a least-busy IBM backend, transpiles to its ISA and
records backend, job identifiers, shots, depth and timing before comparison.
A test-to-test block cannot replace the classifier's test-to-training kernel;
held-out hardware classifier accuracy consequently remains unmeasured.
The eight-qubit embedding is easy to simulate classically. Kernel concentration
can suppress differences between examples, and PCA compression can remove
important distinctions. These small synthetic experiments establish neither
scaling behaviour nor transfer to patient care.
